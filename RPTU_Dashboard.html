RPTU Plant Overview

The Resid Processing and Treating Unit (RPTU) at Numaligarh Refinery Limited (NRL) is designed using Chevron Lummus Global (CLG) LC-FINING and integrated ISOTREATING technologies for processing heavy refinery streams and converting them into valuable lighter products.

Process Description

The RPTU consists of an Ebullated Bed Vacuum Residue Hydrocracking Unit (LC-FINING Unit) fully integrated with a Vacuum Gas Oil (VGO) ISOTREATING Unit (HDT). The unit is designed to process Vacuum Residue (VR), PFCC Clarified Oil, Straight Run Vacuum Gas Oil (SR-VGO), and Heavy Coker Gas Oil (HCGO) feedstocks.

Major Process Sections

  • LC-FINING Section: Ebullated-bed hydrocracking section for processing heavy residue feedstocks such as Vacuum Residue (VR) and PFCC Clarified Oil.
  • Integrated ISOTREATING (HDT) Section: Fixed-bed hydrotreating section designed for treatment of SR-VGO, HCGO and hydrotreated intermediate streams.
  • Hydrogen System: Includes make-up hydrogen and recycle hydrogen systems for maintaining required hydrogen partial pressure during hydroprocessing.
  • Amine Treating Section: Lean amine is used for removal of acid gases such as H₂S from sour gas streams.
  • Injection Water System: Injection water is provided to control salt deposition and corrosion in reactor effluent systems.
  • Fractionation Section: Product separation system for recovery of LPG, naphtha, diesel, hydrotreated VGO and unconverted oil.

Basis for Corrosion Loop Segregation

In accordance with API RP 970 (Corrosion Control Documents) and API 581 (Risk-Based Inspection), the RPTU has been divided into distinct Corrosion Loops. A Corrosion Loop (or Circuit) is defined as a section of the plant that is subject to the same general environment and similar damage mechanisms, allowing for a unified inspection strategy.

Primary Criteria for Loop Boundaries

A new corrosion loop is established when there is a significant change in one or more of the following five parameters:

  • 1. Material of Construction (Metallurgy): Changes in base metal generally trigger a new loop.
  • 2. Process Fluid & Chemistry: Drastic changes in the process environment.
  • 3. Operating Temperature & Pressure: Equipment crossing critical degradation thresholds.
  • 4. Applicable Damage Mechanisms: Unique damage mechanisms mandate separate tracking.
  • 5. Phase Changes & Wash Points: Any point where chemical treatments or wash water is injected.

Applicable Damage Mechanism Matrix

Summary of critical threats to the RPTU equipment and standard mitigation strategies.

Damage Mechanism Description Critical Process Variables Mitigation / Inspection
HTHA
(High Temp Hydrogen Attack)		 Internal decarburization and fissuring of steel caused by hydrogen diffusing into the metal at high temperatures and pressures, forming methane gas blisters. Temperature, Hydrogen Partial Pressure, Time in service. Base metallurgy on API 941 curves (use 2-¼ Cr-1 Mo for reactors). Operate at least 50°F below limits. Inspect using PAUT / TOFD on welds.
High Temp Sulfidation Uniform thinning of carbon and low-alloy steels from reaction with H2S and reactive sulfur compounds at temperatures above 260°C (500°F). Temperature, Total Sulfur content, Presence of Hydrogen. Upgrade to 12Cr (410S) in non-hydrogen services. Upgrade to 347/321 SS in high-temp hydrogen/H2S services. UT thickness monitoring.
Ammonium Bisulfide (NH4HS) Corrosion High-velocity localized erosion-corrosion and under-deposit pitting due to precipitation of NH4HS salts in the reactor effluent coolers. NH4HS concentration in sour water, Fluid Velocity, Wash Water injection rate. Continuous wash water injection (min 25% liquid at cooler inlet). Keep NH4HS < 12 wt%. Use Alloy 825 in REAC. IRIS/Eddy Current on tubes.
Ammonium Chloride (NH4Cl) Corrosion Highly aggressive, localized pitting and under-deposit corrosion occurring when NH4Cl sublimates from vapor into a damp salt in fractionator overheads. Temperature, Chloride concentration, Nitrogen concentration in feed. Limit feed chlorides. Continuous water wash. Upgrade to Alloy 625 cladding in fractionator overheads. Monitor wash water chlorides/pH.
Polythionic Acid SCC (PTASCC) Intergranular cracking of sensitized austenitic stainless steels (300 series) when sulfide scales are exposed to moisture and oxygen during plant shutdowns. Presence of sulfide scale, Oxygen, Moisture, Sensitization of stainless steel. Purge high-temp loops with Nitrogen during shutdown. Apply 2% Soda Ash wash before opening to air. Use stabilized grades (Type 347/321 SS).
Wet H2S Damage (SSC, HIC, SOHIC) Hydrogen blistering, Sulfide Stress Cracking, or Hydrogen Induced Cracking from atomic hydrogen generated by aqueous H2S corrosion entering the steel. H2S concentration in water, pH, Cyanide presence, Temperature. Limit carbon steel hardness to 200 BHN. Mandate PWHT. Use HIC-tested steel. Inspect via WFMT on welds and UT scans.
Temper Embrittlement Loss of metallurgical toughness in heavy-wall 2-¼ Cr-1 Mo reactors after long-term exposure to operating temperatures. Time at temperature, Concentration of tramp elements in steel. Strictly control heating and cooling rates. Do not pressurize vessels >20% until metal reaches Minimum Pressurization Temp (MPT).
Amine Corrosion General or localized thinning in the MDEA treating section, usually caused by dissolved acid gases (H2S, CO2) flashing or high velocities removing the protective film. Amine concentration, Acid gas loading, Velocity, Temperature, Turbulence. Maintain MDEA < 40 wt%. Restrict rich amine velocities (< 6.5 ft/s). Use Type 304L SS in high-velocity/turbulent areas.
Flow Accelerated Corrosion (FAC) Rapid thinning of carbon steel piping in boiler feed water and steam condensate systems due to dissolution of the protective magnetite layer. Fluid velocity, pH, Temperature, Dissolved Oxygen. Upgrade susceptible elbows/tees to low-alloy steel. Control water chemistry. Inspect via RT/UT on outer radii of elbows.
Coking & Under-Deposit Corrosion Thermal degradation of heavy resid (LCF feed) causing coke formation, leading to localized hot spots (in heaters) and under-deposit chemical attack. Conradson Carbon (CCR) in feed, Heater tube skin temperatures, Flow velocity. Monitor feed CCR and asphaltenes. Decoke heater tubes periodically. IR thermography on heater tubes to detect hot spots.
